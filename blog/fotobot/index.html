Research spotlight / Robotic photography & videography

FotoBot: robots as creative partners

Helping people bring creative ideas to life through robots that understand personal intentions and aesthetic preferences.

A creative partner behind the camera

A photograph or a vlog begins with an idea. FotoBot asks how a robot can help turn that idea into something people can create and share.

Capturing a compelling portrait or recording a polished vlog often takes technical skill, careful composition, and coordinated camera movement. The FotoBot series explores how embodied AI can make this creative process more accessible, without requiring everyone to become a professional photographer or filmmaker.

Creative collaboration brings a different set of challenges to robotics: understanding what someone wants to express, responding to subjective aesthetic preferences, and adapting to the world around them. FotoBot connects these questions with autonomous photography and videography in real environments.

Three directions, one creative vision

01 / From intention to photograph

FotoBot: an LLM-integrated prototype

The initial system brings large language models into an embodied photography workflow, exploring how a robot can interpret a user's intent and assist with image capture.

Explore the prototype and paper ↗

02 / Aesthetics in the real world

LEAP: autonomous portrait photography

LEAP explores embodied aesthetics and robotic photography as an urban service. It brings together portrait photography algorithms, adaptive operation in public spaces, and a privacy-preserving workflow that makes image capture and storage transparent to users.

See LEAP and its park deployment ↗

03 / Creativity in motion

Robotic vlog recording

Camera following and motion control extend the project from still images to video. This direction explores how a robot can support vlog recording by moving with people and controlling the camera as a scene unfolds.

Watch the vlog recording demo ↗

Towards everyday creative assistance

The team's future directions include photography systems that respond to free-form instructions, methods for improving and adapting to individual preferences, and a library of camera movements such as dolly shots, orbits, and lateral tracking.

The shared ambition is to make robotic creative assistance an accessible part of everyday life, with people shaping the ideas and robots helping bring them into the world.
